Protest songs use music to express opposition to injustice, war, or political power, and to unite people around a shared cause. They often favor simple melodies and singable refrains so that crowds can join in.

In the United States, folk and gospel traditions fed into civil rights era songs, and artists like Bob Dylan and Aretha Franklin recorded songs that movements adopted as anthems, sometimes beyond the writer's original intent.

In the Philippines, kundiman and later OPM songs have been used to express national feeling and resistance. "Bayan Ko" is a well-known example, rising to prominence in opposition to the Marcos regime and during the 1986 EDSA People Power Revolution.

A song can become a protest song because of how audiences use it, not only because of what its writer intended. When explaining one, it helps to separate the song's own words, its historical moment, and the ways later listeners reused it.

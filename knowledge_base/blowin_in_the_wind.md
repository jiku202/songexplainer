Bob Dylan wrote "Blowin' in the Wind" in 1962, and it was released in 1963 on The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan. A cover by Peter, Paul and Mary the same year became a major hit and spread the song further.

The song is made of open-ended questions about war, freedom, and indifference, and its refrain suggests the answer is hard to pin down.

It became closely associated with the American civil rights movement and the wider protest-song tradition.

Readings listeners offer: a call to confront injustice; a statement that the answers are obvious but ignored; a deliberately open question that lets each listener supply their own answer.

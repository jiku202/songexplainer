A metaphor describes one thing as another to reveal a feeling or idea, such as treating a person as a place of shelter. A simile makes the comparison explicit using "like" or "as." Both help a songwriter express emotion without stating it flatly.

Imagery uses concrete sensory details (sights, sounds, textures) so the listener experiences a moment instead of being told about it. Strong imagery often lets a song feel specific while still being relatable.

Repetition, especially in a chorus or refrain, makes a central idea memorable and can change meaning when the surrounding verses change. A title phrase repeated in different contexts is a common technique.

Point of view and persona: the narrator of a song is not always the songwriter. Writers often create a character, and listeners should separate the speaker from the artist.

Ambiguity is a deliberate tool. Leaving a line open to more than one reading lets different listeners connect the song to their own lives, which is why many songs support several valid interpretations.

Song structure (verse, chorus, bridge) carries meaning too. A bridge often shifts perspective or raises the stakes, and a change in arrangement can underline a change in emotion.

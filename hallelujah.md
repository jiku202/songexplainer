"Hallelujah" first appeared on Leonard Cohen's 1984 album Various Positions. Cohen reportedly wrote many draft verses, and different recordings and covers use different selections of them. John Cale's version and Jeff Buckley's 1994 recording helped make it widely known.

Themes: the overlap of the sacred and the erotic, love that ends in pain, and praise offered even when things are broken.

It alludes to biblical figures such as David and Samson, which gives the song its mix of worship and human failure.

Readings listeners offer: a song about a failed relationship; a meditation on faith and doubt; a statement that a "broken" hallelujah can still be sincere.
